# Family Tree

Family Tree is a Store application for building private family trees with Apps Hub profiles. It runs in a desktop window and at `/pub/familytree/` when its public page is enabled in Apps Hub settings. Version 1.0.0 is its first official release.

Each profile sees the trees it owns and the trees shared with it. Create a tree, add its first person, and connect relatives by selecting up to two parents. A person's name is required; their sex, story, birth year, exact birth date and Apps Hub profile link are optional. Sex may be male, female or not specified; existing records retain their data and default to not specified. An exact birth date supplies its year automatically. If both values are supplied, their years must match. Mark a person as **Deceased** to record a death year or exact death date, or leave both dates unknown. The death year must agree with its date and cannot precede birth. Cards show a life range, such as `1941 – 2022` or `1967 – present`; a deceased person with an unknown death date is clearly marked. Full dates appear in the detail panel, together with current age or age at death. Completed years use full calendar dates; babies are shown in months or days. Values based on partial years are marked as approximate. Missing birth dates or unknown death dates do not produce a guessed age.

People can have multiple partners and spouses, including former partners or former spouses. From a person's details, **Add a new partner** creates another person linked to them. In the editor, **Link an existing person** selects a person already in this tree and the relationship type. Saving a relationship updates both records in one transaction. Parent links remain separate: shared children identify co-parents without assuming marriage. **Add child** fills in the second parent when exactly one partner or co-parent is known; review both parents before saving. Labels such as father, mother, son, daughter, partner, former partner, husband and wife follow the referenced person’s recorded sex. Gendered birth, death and lifespan wording also follows the person’s sex, with neutral descriptions when it is unknown. Partner selectors update their labels when a different person is chosen; languages with a common term retain that term. Male person cards are blue, female cards are pink, and unspecified records keep the neutral green styling. Sex labels remain in person details and the editor; cards distinguish sex through color and use the space for a larger name, current age or age at death, and life dates. Relative buttons show how much older or younger each person is compared with the person currently open. These differences use birth dates, even when someone has died; matching dates or matching partial years are identified separately. Partner lines are gold; former relationships use dashed lines.

A person cannot be their own ancestor. Parent and partner links can use a person from another accessible tree, with a live reference to the original record. Trees support up to 2,000 people.

## Linked trees

In **Add person**, choose **Use an existing person** to select someone from another tree you own or can access. In a person editor, **Find parents or partners in another tree** adds that tree's people to the parent and partner selectors. Saving creates references for the selected foreign people without copying their personal data or editing their original tree. Editors submit these operations for review under the destination tree's normal rules.

The person editor lists existing tree memberships by name and role. Add the same original person to multiple accessible trees, open a linked tree, or remove an individual membership after confirmation. Removing a membership also removes relationships recorded for that person in the destination, while preserving the original record and other memberships. Controls respect destination permissions: viewers cannot change links, editors submit proposals, and owners/administrators apply changes directly. Link changes save separately from the biography form and preserve its draft. Existing partner relationships derived from another tree show the partner name, relationship type and source tree; they are edited at their source rather than silently duplicated locally. Only authorized tree names and memberships are returned.

A tree marker on a person opens the linked tree directly, replacing the current tree. The linked person is highlighted at 100% zoom in the destination. If more than one linked tree is available for a person, including a referenced person, choose the destination from a list. The sidebar’s **Linked trees** button lists connected trees to open. Only one tree is displayed at a time; there are no combined regions or visibility checkboxes.

The single-tree view also shows incoming partners and children from accessible linked trees, including the other parent of a shared child even when no partnership is recorded. Thus a family connection is visible from either tree without copying records or inserting reciprocal memberships. Foreign people carry an original-tree marker; opening it switches trees rather than moving the person between regions. Unrelated people remain in their own tree. Relationships derived from another tree are edited where they were created; saving local notes does not silently store these derived relationships. Names alone never merge unrelated records. A view supports up to 8,000 people.

Every viewer needs access to each original tree whose personal data they see. Linking a person does not grant access or elevate editing rights. Foreign details are edited using **Open original tree**, under that tree's own role. If access is revoked, the destination shows an unavailable placeholder without the person's private attributes. **Remove from this tree** removes the reference and its local connections; it preserves the original person. Deleting an original person or tree removes its references and affected ancestry links, with version checks preventing stale proposals from recreating deleted relationships.

The **View** button switches directly between the default **Graphical tree** and the optional **Compact list**, without a selection dialog. Its tooltip names the destination view. The choice is saved in the app SQLite database separately for each authenticated Apps Hub profile, applies across trees and devices, and is available to viewers as well as contributors. New profiles start with the graphical tree. The compact view is an alphabetically sorted, searchable list with age, life dates and clickable parent, partner and child relationships. It includes counts of visible people, parent–child links and recorded partnerships. Select a person to show conventional kinship labels relative to them; follow a relative to select and scroll to that person. Separate info and linked-tree buttons retain the usual details and navigation. On phones, rows stack within the screen width and the list scrolls normally without canvas zoom or dragging. Only people and relationships authorized by the existing tree view are included.

Opening a tree starts at exactly 100% zoom. If a visible person is linked to the authenticated Apps Hub profile, that person is highlighted and centered; matching uses the authenticated profile ID, never a name or tree ownership. Otherwise a founder in the selected tree is horizontally centered near the top, below the search box, leaving room for descendants underneath; the choice prefers the top generation and oldest known birth year. The **100%** button restores the exact natural size around the current view center without preventing further zooming. Opening a linked person’s tree starts at 100% and highlights that person. Drag the canvas to explore. Scroll or pinch to zoom, use **Fit tree** to see the whole tree, or search by name to focus on a person. Select a card to highlight that person and their direct connections without opening a panel. Parent-to-child lines display animated arrows; partner links display arrows in both directions. Incoming parent links are blue, outgoing child links are green, and partner links are gold. Other connections fade while a person is selected. This includes visible family references from other trees. Select the same card again to clear the highlighting. There is no floating relationship legend, and the selected card has no redundant kinship caption. Reduced-motion preferences keep the arrows visible without animation.

Each visible card shows its relationship to the selected person: parents, children, grandparents, grandchildren, siblings, known half-siblings, aunts/uncles, nieces/nephews, cousins and more distant ancestors or descendants. Cards display a single supported kinship term. Unsupported, ambiguous and unconnected relationships have no kinship badge; raw relationship chains are never shown. Bulgarian uncles distinguish the paternal and maternal sides. Chinese labels use the known family side and, where required, birth order; Japanese sibling labels require known birth order. Culturally specific terms that cannot be determined reliably are omitted. Calculations use only authorized people loaded in the current view, never names or hidden records. A missing second parent does not imply a half-sibling. All relationship labels and controls have translations in the nine supported languages.

Use the separate **ⓘ** button to read the biography, open relatives, edit a person or add a child. When you follow relatives from one person to another, **Back** returns through those people and restores the previous tree position and information scroll position. Opening another person with their information button starts a new navigation history. Keyboard users can focus the canvas and use arrow keys to move, plus/minus to zoom and zero to fit. Use **Refresh** to fetch changes made in another window or on another device. The canvas occupies the full height without a top toolbar. The sidebar has one compact row of icon buttons for **New tree**, **Linked trees** and **Refresh**, with translated tooltips and accessible labels. Linked trees is disabled when the current tree has no linked trees. **Add person** is available in the selected tree’s options menu. Right-click a tree in the sidebar, press Shift+F10 while it is focused, or use its **⋯** button to open that tree’s options. The menu provides opening the tree, refreshing, adding a person, reviewing changes, sharing and settings as permitted by the user’s role. Actions on another tree first open that tree; settings include renaming and deletion. On phones and in narrow desktop windows, the floating **☰** button opens the **Trees** drawer. Close it with the × button, by tapping the shaded background, or with Escape. Choosing a tree or starting a new one also closes the drawer. The drawer keeps keyboard focus while open, and the underlying tree becomes available again as soon as it closes.

Linking an Apps Hub profile identifies a person and displays their profile name and username. It does not grant access, copy the person's biography, or reveal the tree to that profile. Sharing is a separate action.

## Sharing and review

The owner or an administrator opens **Share**, finds an Apps Hub profile and selects a role. Shared trees appear in that profile's tree list.

- **Viewer:** reads the tree and person details.
- **Editor:** submits additions, edits and removals as proposals. The existing tree stays unchanged until approval. Editors see their own proposals and review results.
- **Administrator:** edits directly, reviews proposals, manages sharing, renames the tree and can delete the entire tree. Administrators have the owner's management rights, except that no one can change or remove the owner's role.

**Changes** shows pending proposals and the latest 300 relevant change records, with before/after values, contributor names, comments and review outcomes. The owner or an administrator can approve or reject a proposal. Approvals recheck the contributor's current access, the target person's version, profile existence, ancestry and partner links. Partner edits increment the versions of both people; outdated proposals cannot silently overwrite a relationship edited from the other person's record. An outdated proposal is rejected by the server without overwriting newer edits; reject it and submit a fresh proposal after refreshing the tree. An editor whose access was removed or reduced to viewer cannot have outstanding changes approved.

Removing a person keeps their children, removes references to that person as a parent or partner, and updates the affected children's versions. Deleting a tree permanently deletes its people, memberships and history. Administrators can do this as well as the owner, so grant that role only deliberately.

## Data and implementation

All persistent data belongs to `apps/familytree/data.db`. The public API lives in `apps/familytree/api.py`; the shared desktop/web UI and all nine translations live in `apps/familytree/public/`. No premium or privileged backend module is needed. Every API request requires an Apps Hub session, except for the page shell, and every tree operation checks membership on the server. Tree changes and approvals run inside SQLite write transactions.

Run API tests from the installation root with `venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s apps/familytree/tests -v`. Tests use temporary databases and fictional profiles, leaving installation data untouched.

Calendar age tests run with `node apps/familytree/tests/test_dates.cjs`. They cover birthdays, leap days, infants, partial dates, missing dates and differences between living and deceased relatives.

Relationship inference tests run with `node apps/familytree/tests/test_kinship.cjs`, covering ancestry, cousins, half-siblings, marriage paths, disconnected records, redaction, identity and cycle handling.


## Premium statistics

The sidebar **Statistics** button opens a responsive dashboard for the selected tree or **All accessible trees**. Desktop always offers the button and uses the mvmOS Premium subscription dialog when the module is missing. Apps Hub public pages omit the button entirely without the Premium module; public profiles are never offered a purchase prompt. The base API contains only feature detection, code delivery and delegation. Calculations and the dashboard live in `apps/familytree/premium/` and are absent from the free app archive. Missing Premium returns 402 for statistics and a non-cacheable 404 for the dashboard script.

The dashboard includes people recorded as living or deceased, known ages, visible parent/partner links, longest known ancestral chain, average living age, average completed lifespan and approximate parental age at childbirth with sample counts. Top-ten lists show oldest and youngest living people, longest lives including living people, most known children and most recorded partners (including former relationships). Charts show living age groups, recorded sex, birth decades, living birth months and data completeness. Click a ranked person or birthday to open their original tree and details.

A single-tree report uses its authorized visible people, including accessible linked family previews. All-tree reports use unique original person IDs across accessible trees, so references do not duplicate someone. Private and redacted records are excluded, and access is rechecked for each report. “Living” means not marked deceased; missing values are never inferred. Exact date ages use completed years, year-only ages are marked approximate, and averages use known values only. Death-date completeness uses deceased people as its denominator.

The birthday calendar offers the next 30 days, 90 days or 12 months, including birthdays today, for living people with exact birth dates. It shows the coming age. For calendar purposes February 29 is observed on February 28 in non-leap years, explicitly stated in the interface. Names, labels, explanations and dates are localized in all nine supported languages.


## Adoptive parent relationships and marriage statistics

When adding or editing a person, each parent selector has an **Adopted by this parent** checkbox. Adoption is recorded separately for each parent in `adopted_parents` alongside the existing `parents` IDs, allowing one adoptive and one unmarked parent. The control is disabled without a selected parent. Unmarked relationships are not asserted to be biological. Adoptive relationships remain part of the family layout and kinship graph, with dashed parent lines and localized direct adoptive-parent/adopted-child labels in details, selection captions and the compact list. Changing a flag follows the tree's existing role, proposal, review and version rules. Deleting or unlinking a parent removes the associated adoption metadata; redacted or inaccessible parent metadata is not exposed. Existing records without flags remain valid.

Premium statistics additionally show unique adopted children, unique adoptive parents and adoption edges, with adopted-child subtotals in the largest-family ranking. A child adopted by two parents counts once as a child and twice as a relationship. Adoptive edges are excluded from the average parental age at childbirth, since birth age does not describe the age at adoption.

Marriage statistics count unique current spouse pairs when both people are recorded as living, unique former-marriage pairs, and living people with or without a recorded current marriage. Children are included in that latter population. Missing marriage records are not labeled as proof that someone is unmarried; partnerships and shared children do not establish marriage. Authorized single-tree and all-tree scopes, deduplication and Premium gating apply to these additions as well.


From a statistics result, person details include **Back to statistics**. This remains available when following relatives, alongside the existing person-history Back button. Returning reloads authorized statistics and restores the selected tree/all-tree scope, birthday period, dashboard scroll position and the birthday list’s own scroll position. The original underlying tree view is restored as well. State lives only in the current navigation; statistics HTML or private report data is not cached for restoration.
